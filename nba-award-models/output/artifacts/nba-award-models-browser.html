Methodology

Current model notes followed by the original methodology guide.

All awards · exponential availability

Every award applies a strictly negative fitted weight to an exponential curve of games below ceil(85% of actual team season games). At or above threshold the added contribution is zero. An 82-game season requires 70 appearances, a 72-game season 62, and a 66-game season 57.

Let d be games below threshold, S team season games, and r = ln(2)/(0.10 × S). The penalty is a negative fitted weight × [exp(r × d) − 1 − r × d]/r. Removing the linear component softens small shortfalls while retaining exponential growth for large absences. The same curve applies to every award; each award learns its own negative strength from prior seasons.

The curves were revised after inspecting 2016–17 ROY and 2022–23 MVP. Their results are retrospective. It replaces the linear shortfall penalty. Other coefficients are refitted with existing settings and candidate rules. Existing G terms remain where present; MIP displays G for context. Players are not newly excluded below 50%.

Multi-team rows use a flagged season-maximum schedule approximation. The original guide below predates these revisions. Results remain retrospective.

DPOY & Sixth Man · team context

DPOY adds NBA regular-season team defensive-rating rank and team wins, and removes player defensive rebounds (DRB). The defensive-rank coefficient is negative and wins coefficient nonnegative. Team DEF_RATING is separate from the retained player DRtg input. There is no hard top-five cutoff.

Sixth Man adds reported conference seed with a negative coefficient. Missing or zero historical seeds, and the incomplete 1999–00 standings feed, use record-based conference rank as a flagged fallback. There is no hard top-four cutoff. Aggregate multi-team rows use the season candidate-pool median for missing team context.

NBA advanced team ratings from 1996–97 onward are cached via the sportsdataverse NBA Stats archive, selecting Regular Season. Earlier training uses a historical Basketball Reference archive. All 1,177 team records reconcile to cached standings.

Current MVP · qualified top-five penalties

MVP candidates must qualify for both Basketball Reference’s PPG and WS/48 leaderboards. Below fifth, each statistic receives a separately fitted negative exponential penalty. Top-five positions have zero rank penalty. The curve uses d=max(0,rank−5), N=qualified leaderboard size, and [exp(r×d)−1]/r. PPG uses r=ln(2)/(0.05×N); WS/48 uses r=ln(2)/(0.10×N). These curve values are not standardized or centered. Ranks are determined league-wide before MVP screening.

Cached source qualification flags and full-precision values are used where available. Other seasons use documented source qualification rules, local total points divided by games, and local rounded WS/48. Tied rounded WS/48 values share the best competition rank. This means the exact WS/48 order can differ from the website when hidden precision breaks a displayed tie.

The model adds actual team wins minus preseason expected wins. Expected wins use preseason market win totals, adjusted to actual team schedule length; only the difference is a new fitted input. Its coefficient is constrained nonnegative so outperforming expectations helps, holding other inputs fixed.

PER replaces BPM. Games below ceil(85% of the team’s actual regular-season games) receive an exponentially increasing negative availability contribution. Existing inputs, including G, remain. The half-life is 20 seasons and regularization 0.03.

Preseason lines are available from 2002–03. Earlier training seasons have a neutral zero expectation feature. Multi-team rows use the eligible season-pool median for missing team inputs. Uneven schedules use preseason expected win rate multiplied by actual games played. Source pages, URLs and hashes are cached locally.

Historical predictions train on prior seasons. Results are retrospective; 2010–11 through 2014–15 overlaps the original settings-selection period. The original guide below describes the earlier BPM snapshot.

02 / Scores and coefficients
02 / Scores and coefficients Step 1: compare like with like For each award and season, first select its candidate pool. Missing feature values are replaced by that pool's median. Each feature is then standardized using the pool's mean and population standard deviation: z = (player value - candidate-pool average) / candidate-pool standard deviation A z-score of +1 means one standard deviation above the pool average; -1 means one below. This makes points, percentages and advanced statistics comparable and partly adjusts for changes in statistical eras. It does not remove every difference between eras. Step 2: add the weighted statistics Player score = coefficient[1] x z[1] + ... + coefficient[p] x z[p] A coefficient is the score change from a one-standard-deviation increase in that feature, holding the other standardized inputs fixed. Coefficients are not percentages, raw-stat point values or causal effects. Larger absolute coefficients imply a larger conditional score change for the same standardized movement. Worked example using MVP scoring The revised MVP points coefficient is +1.270822. Suppose, purely for illustration, that the pool average is 12 points per game and its standard deviation is 6. A player scoring 24 points has z = (24 - 12) / 6 = 2. The scoring term adds 2 x 1.270822 = 2.541645 to the player's score. These pool statistics are hypothetical; calculations use unrounded coefficients. Step 3: convert scores into model shares Share(i) = exp(score(i)) / sum of exp(score(k)) across candidates This is a softmax calculation. It preserves the score ranking and produces positive shares adding to 100%. A player's share changes when rivals change. A share of 40% has not been demonstrated to mean a 40% real-world chance of winning. Reading negative coefficients A negative coefficient lowers the fitted score as that input rises, conditional on the other inputs. Lower defensive rating is desirable, so its negative DPOY coefficient is intuitive. Other negative signs may reflect overlapping statistics, limited winner samples and model specification. They do not show that worse efficiency or fewer games causes awards. For two players, their model-share ratio is exp(score A - score B). A one-standard-deviation scoring advantage alone gives a revised MVP ratio multiplier of exp(1.270822), about 3.56. This is a model-relative comparison, not a guaranteed probability increase. 03 / Training and recency
03 / Training and recency What the fitting procedure learns The feature lists were chosen by the analyst. The coefficients were then learned by minimizing weighted historical prediction loss. Each season forms one contest, so modern seasons do not receive extra training weight simply because they have larger rosters. Tied winners split the target outcome equally. Objective = weighted average of -sum(target share x log(model share)) + (lambda / 2) x sum(coefficient squared) The first term rewards assigning share to actual winners. The second is L2 regularization: it discourages excessively large coefficients and helps limit overfitting. There is no intercept because adding the same constant to every player's score in a contest cancels in softmax. How newer seasons receive more weight Season weight = 2 ^ (-(latest training year - season year) / half-life) Weights are normalized to sum to one within each training run. This changes how historical outcomes influence the learned coefficients. It does not discount a current player's statistics or automatically place more emphasis on recent games within a season. 10 / Data and practical use
10 / Data and practical use Data preparation and eligibility PlayerID plus Season is unique in the selected CSV. Traded-player records were not summed; some team labels denote multiple teams. MP is total minutes, so minutes per game is MP/G. Corrupted character encoding in names was repaired in memory, then accents and punctuation normalized for winner matching. Every winner in each modeled season was required to appear in the candidate pool. The 20-game minimum is an analyst screening rule. Official award-specific games/minutes requirements, exceptions and appeals are not fully implemented. Current eligibility needs a separate check before treating a ranking as an actionable forecast. Main limitations MVP now includes team wins and league/conference standing. Team defensive ranking, tracking defense, injuries, reputation, voting narratives, prior award voting and market odds remain absent. Correlated inputs can produce unintuitive coefficients. There are no missing-value indicator features; median imputation assigns neutral within-pool values without identifying missingness. Using the models on another season Apply the same candidate filters and calculate every feature with the same units. Calculate pool medians, means and population standard deviations for that season, then standardize. If a feature is constant, the implementation uses a standard deviation of one. Multiply by the saved coefficients, add the terms and apply stable softmax. Rank shares from highest to lowest. New-season ranking accuracy remains unproven until evaluated. How conference standing is calculated ESPN historical feeds supply conference membership and team records for all 41 modeled seasons (1,177 team-seasons). Sources & notes

Where the comparisons come from and how to read them.

Player statistics

Season statistics come from the repository’s basketball_stats.csv. Each player stats table shows the inputs used by the selected award model, before within-season standardization. Percentage inputs are displayed as percentages. The player table displays actual league and conference positions by regular-season record; ties use average rank. The model uses normalized 0–1 standings internally. These positions do not apply playoff seeding tiebreakers.

Actual award results

Top-three finishers and voting points come from Basketball Reference’s season award tables, cached locally for 2001–2026. Every season comparison links to its voting source. Tied third places are included.

Preseason expectations

Basketball Reference preseason win-total market lines supply expected wins. The model uses actual wins minus schedule-adjusted expected wins. For 2016–17 OKC, the preseason line was 45 and actual wins were 47: a +2 difference. View preseason source. Lines begin in 2002–03; earlier training seasons contribute no expectation signal.

Reading the scores

Model scores are uncalibrated conditional model shares. Voting points are the actual award ballot totals. These use different scales. The stats table includes each distinct player from either top three, with both ranks shown where available.

Leaderboard qualification & rank penalties

MVP candidates must qualify for both PPG and WS/48. Ranks below fifth receive separately fitted exponential negative adjustments. The PPG marginal penalty doubles each further 5% of its qualified field below fifth; WS/48 uses 10%. The top five receive zero. Cached exact source ranks are used where available. Uncached seasons use documented qualifications and local data; rounded WS/48 ties share the best competition rank. Basketball Reference qualification requirements.

Availability for all five awards

Every model applies a strictly negative fitted weight to an exponential shortfall curve below ceil(85% of actual team season games). The curve removes its linear component for a gentler start, retaining exponential growth for large shortfalls; each award fits its own strength on prior seasons. The added contribution is zero at or above threshold. Multi-team schedule approximations are flagged; existing games-played features remain where present.

Missing inputs & eligibility

An asterisk marks an input filled with the eligible candidate pool’s median, as used by the model. A player outside the model’s candidate pool is labeled. Candidate screening starts at 20 games; official award eligibility is not fully implemented.

Validation

Historical predictions train on earlier seasons. The 2000–01 through 2014–15 seasons were part of historical training or settings development; they are not an untouched test. The MVP revision is a retrospective comparison after later outcomes were inspected. Current full-history coefficients are shown separately in model settings.
